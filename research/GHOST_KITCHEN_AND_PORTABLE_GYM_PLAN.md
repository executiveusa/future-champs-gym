# Ghost Kitchen and Portable Gym Operating Plan

## Status

This is internal strategy. It does not authorize public claims, food sales, donations, raffles, youth programming, or delivery-platform integrations.

## A Taste of Nawlins model

A Taste of Nawlins should be positioned as a **portable Southern food concept** that can operate through approved commissary kitchens, licensed pop-ups, food carts, catered events, and later delivery marketplaces.

Public language:

> A Taste of Nawlins is a portable food fundraiser. Follow the schedule to see where the next menu day will appear.

Do not claim active Uber Eats, DoorDash, Grubhub, or other marketplace availability until the operator account, menu, kitchen, permits, service area, fees, fulfillment process, and live listing are verified.

## Recommended operating sequence

1. Confirm legal operator and Washington business registration.
2. Confirm county/city food permitting path for each sales format.
3. Choose a permitted commissary kitchen.
4. Cost a five-item menu.
5. Test production timing and food holding.
6. Run a controlled preorder pickup with a hard capacity limit.
7. Reconcile revenue, food cost, refunds, waste, labor, and program allocation.
8. Repeat successfully before adding delivery platforms.
9. Add one marketplace at a time and compare unit economics.

## Initial verified kitchen leads

### Mount Vernon Commissary Kitchen

- City-supported kitchen incubator serving chefs, caterers, food trucks, bakers, market vendors, and other food producers.
- Published flex pricing includes five-hour and eight-hour blocks.
- Strong geographic fit for Mount Vernon, Arlington, and Skagit County testing.
- Verify current availability, permitted uses, storage, insurance, onboarding, and delivery-driver access directly.

### Seattle Commissary Kitchen

- Membership-based shared-use operator with multiple Seattle locations.
- Publishes kitchen equipment, storage, scheduling, licensing support, and plans suitable for food trucks, catering, meal prep, packaged foods, and delivery apps.
- Strong option for Seattle demand testing, but farther from Arlington.
- Verify which locations currently accept members and calculate transport and labor impact.

### KBM Seattle Commissary Kitchen

- West Seattle shared licensed kitchen with published plans beginning around small cart/farmers-market use and scaling to larger workstations.
- Potential fit for periodic Seattle production or event support.
- Verify utilities, deposits, insurance, permits, storage, driver pickup, and schedule.

### K Commissary

- Rainier Valley shared kitchen with monthly rental and broad access outside a weekday block.
- Potential fit for Seattle delivery or catering production.
- Verify membership, storage, onboarding, current rates, permit compatibility, and loading access.

## Partnership scoring model

Score each kitchen from 1–5 on:

- distance to customers;
- hourly or monthly cost;
- minimum commitment;
- permit support;
- equipment fit;
- refrigeration/freezer/dry storage;
- parking and loading;
- delivery-driver access;
- schedule flexibility;
- cleaning burden;
- insurance requirements;
- community and business support;
- ability to host pickup;
- ability to support food carts or mobile units.

Select a kitchen only after a live tour and written terms review.

## Delivery marketplace strategy

### Phase 1 — direct preorder

- Website email and SMS consent.
- Limited menu.
- Fixed service day.
- Fixed pickup windows.
- Hard order cap.
- Direct customer relationship.

### Phase 2 — direct local delivery

- Small delivery radius.
- Fixed route windows.
- Minimum order or family trays.
- Validate packaging, freshness, route time, and refund handling.

### Phase 3 — one marketplace pilot

- Choose one platform based on geographic reach and economics.
- Publish only when active.
- Measure fees, promotions, refunds, missing items, delivery time, customer ownership, and repeat rate.
- Do not launch multiple marketplaces before one is operationally stable.

## Website menu model

Each menu item must have:

- name;
- short description;
- status: testing, confirmed, sold out, seasonal, unavailable;
- serving size;
- price after approval;
- allergen statement after review;
- order limit;
- available service date;
- pickup/delivery method;
- photograph status: real, approved, current.

The menu page should always be useful even when no sale is open:

- show what is being tested;
- show the next announcement date when confirmed;
- collect menu interest;
- let subscribers choose preferred items and service area;
- never present a pending item as available.

## Email and supporter system

Collect only what is needed:

- adult name;
- email;
- optional phone;
- city or ZIP code;
- interests: food, youth program, volunteering, sponsorship, equipment support;
- explicit update consent;
- source and timestamp.

### Incentives

Permitted, low-risk incentives may include:

- first access to menus;
- subscriber-only recipe stories;
- early pickup windows;
- thank-you offers after purchase;
- lawful giveaways with approved official rules.

Do not run a raffle, sweepstakes, or donation-linked prize without legal review and written rules.

## Portable youth boxing gym categories

Final brands, sizes, quantities, and safety suitability require the coach and youth-program safety reviewer.

### Individual participant kit

- properly sized training gloves;
- hand wraps;
- mouthguard and storage case;
- headgear when required by the training activity;
- groin or chest protection as appropriate;
- jump rope;
- water bottle;
- washable gear bag;
- program shirt;
- cleaning and hygiene supplies.

### Coach and shared training kit

- focus mitts;
- kick or body shields only if curriculum includes them;
- punch paddles;
- interval timer;
- cones and agility markers;
- resistance bands;
- medicine balls by age-appropriate weight;
- speed rope assortment;
- portable floor markers;
- first-aid kit;
- instant cold packs;
- sanitation supplies;
- equipment inventory labels;
- lockable storage bins;
- folding cart or wagon;
- scale only if operationally necessary and handled with youth-safe policy.

### Facility-dependent equipment

Do not buy until storage, installation, supervision, and liability are confirmed:

- heavy bags;
- bag stands or mounts;
- double-end bags;
- speed bags and platforms;
- ring equipment;
- floor mats;
- benches;
- mirrors;
- strength equipment.

## Amazon wish-list rule

Do not publish a generic shopping list as a safety recommendation. The coach must approve exact age ranges, sizes, weights, materials, and quantities. The list should be divided into:

- essential participant kits;
- shared coaching equipment;
- sanitation and storage;
- facility-dependent equipment;
- gift cards or flexible equipment fund.

Before publishing:

- verify seller and current listing;
- avoid counterfeit or unknown protective equipment;
- confirm return policy;
- confirm no affiliate relationship unless disclosed;
- remove purchased duplicates;
- protect recipient address through Amazon wish-list privacy controls;
- state that buying an item is not automatically tax deductible.

## Automation and cron plan

No automated public publishing or purchasing.

Recommended scheduled jobs after the production stack exists:

- Daily: form-delivery health check and failed-submission alert.
- Daily: backup verification.
- Daily: broken-link and primary-route test.
- Weekly: menu status review reminder.
- Weekly: consent and email-delivery health report.
- Weekly: Beads stale-task and blocker summary.
- Weekly: social content queue review; human approval required before publishing.
- Monthly: dependency, security, accessibility, performance, and data-retention review.
- After each event: revenue reconciliation, incident check, customer feedback, and impact evidence package.

Cron jobs may prepare drafts and reports. They may not publish legal claims, move money, create marketplace promotions, purchase products, or release youth content without human approval.
