# Future Champs — The Corner Identity Territories

Status: Founder review draft

## Governing idea

**Every young person deserves someone in their corner.**

The identity must translate the boxing corner into a human promise: guidance, protection, recovery, discipline, strategy, encouragement, and truth.

## Locked family

- Master identity: red, white, and black institutional championship
- Current design phase: black and white only
- Heritage accent: restrained gold, deferred until the core marks are approved
- Fundraiser identity: A Taste of Nawlins remains a separate endorsed sub-brand

## Territory 01 — Wordmark-led

### Idea

A custom FUTURE CHAMPS wordmark carries the institution. The typography is broad, direct, and durable. A small corner cut or structural interruption creates a proprietary detail without becoming decorative.

### Strengths

- Highest clarity for parents, sponsors, partners, and public documents
- Strong website and signage performance
- Easily reproduced in one color
- Establishes Future Champs as an institution rather than a fight promotion

### Risk

Without custom lettering, it could become generic. The final wordmark must be drawn, not merely typed.

### Best applications

Website header, forms, sponsor decks, facility signage, uniforms, legal documents, press materials.

## Territory 02 — FC Corner monogram-led

### Idea

A compact FC symbol is constructed from the geometry of a boxing-ring corner and two horizontal rope lines. The mark should feel protective and directional rather than aggressive.

### Strengths

- Strong memory marker
- Works at favicon and social-avatar size
- Suitable for embroidery, gloves, hats, wraps, and equipment labels
- Connects the emotional platform to a recognizable symbol

### Risk

The geometry must remain readable as both F and C. Over-compression would turn it into an abstract sports mark.

### Best applications

Favicon, social avatar, apparel, boxing gear, mobile navigation, pattern system, motion identity.

## Territory 03 — Corner Frame system-led

### Idea

The identity extends beyond a logo into a framing system. An open corner shape holds photography, headlines, milestones, menus, and calls to action. The frame represents the people and structure surrounding a young person.

### Strengths

- Creates a recognizable system without excessive branding
- Gives The Corner blog a proprietary editorial device
- Works across food, youth programming, fundraising, and progress reporting
- Provides expressive range while keeping the logo restrained

### Risk

The frame cannot become a decorative box used everywhere. It should appear only when it clarifies hierarchy or reinforces the brand promise.

### Best applications

The Corner blog, documentary photography, social templates, campaign posters, menu drops, milestone reports, presentations.

## Recommended synthesis

Do not choose one territory and discard the others. Build one coherent system:

1. **Primary wordmark** — FUTURE CHAMPS
2. **Compact symbol** — FC Corner monogram
3. **Expressive device** — Corner Frame
4. **Secondary heritage seal** — developed only after the first three survive pressure testing

## Pressure-test requirements

Every proposed mark must work in:

- black on white
- white on black
- 16-pixel favicon
- 24-pixel mobile header
- one-inch embroidery
- single-color vinyl cut
- photocopied forms
- social avatar crop
- boxing glove application
- website hero
- sponsor deck cover
- food menu endorsement

## Rejection criteria

Reject any direction that depends on:

- gradients, chrome, bevels, glow, or shadows
- boxer silhouettes or aggressive fighting poses
- detailed gloves inside the primary mark
- stars, crowns, shields, and slogans competing in one badge
- tiny circular wording
- patriotic red-white-blue league styling
- AI-generated raster artwork presented as a production logo
- a font used without meaningful customization

## Founder review questions

1. Does this feel like Glenn and the future institution he wants to build?
2. Does “someone in your corner” express the heart of the program?
3. Should the identity feel more protective, more disciplined, or more competitive?
4. Which matters most at first glance: the name FUTURE CHAMPS or the FC symbol?
5. Is there any family or boxing-history element that must eventually appear in the heritage seal?
6. Does anything feel false, overly polished, or disconnected from Northwest Washington?

## Decision gate

The next phase begins only after founder approval of:

- The Corner as the central strategic idea
- the wordmark + monogram + frame architecture
- the protective, institutional tone
- the black-and-white geometry direction

Color, merchandise mockups, and final production vectors remain deferred until that approval.
