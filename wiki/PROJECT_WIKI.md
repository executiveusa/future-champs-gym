## Project thesis

Future Champs is being developed as a youth boxing and mentorship organization in Northwest Washington. Its first revenue experiment is A Taste of Nawlins, a portable food fundraiser designed to operate through permitted pop-ups, commissary kitchens, licensed event setups, or future delivery-platform integrations. Its first program experiment is The First Five, a small youth pilot intended to generate real evidence before expansion.

## Design challenge

How might Future Champs use a disciplined food fundraiser and a small, safe youth boxing pilot to build trust, revenue, community participation, and verified evidence across the Seattle-to-Arlington corridor without overstating its current maturity?

## Human-centered design basis

The work follows Inspiration, Ideation, and Implementation:

- Inspiration: interview Glenn, families, gym operators, food operators, local partners, and potential customers in context.
- Ideation: synthesize themes, create insight statements, define design principles, prototype offers and messages.
- Implementation: live-test a limited fundraiser and pilot, define success, monitor results, and iterate.

## Systems view

### Stocks

- community trust;
- cash and operating reserve;
- customer email list;
- sponsor relationships;
- verified program knowledge;
- reusable brand and software assets;
- founder credibility;
- youth-safety capability.

### Flows

- food sales;
- donations or equipment support after approval;
- volunteer and sponsor leads;
- content and founder storytelling;
- program sessions;
- customer and parent feedback;
- expenses, maintenance, and reputation gain/loss.

### Feedback loops

- food quality → customer trust → repeat orders → more pilot funding;
- verified sessions → credible stories → sponsor trust → more program capacity;
- clear brand → recognition → stronger social response → more local partnerships;
- excessive scope → operational failure → trust loss — controlled through portfolio limits.

## Key decisions

- The master brand is Future Champs.
- A Taste of Nawlins is a related but separate food identity.
- The food operation is portable; “ghost kitchen” describes the operating model, not a confirmed delivery-platform relationship.
- The first youth program remains limited to five participants until systems are verified.
- The public site must show current reality and future vision separately.
- Logo design follows strategy, architecture, and black-and-white vector testing.

## Current platform posture

## Human approval gates

Required before publication or transaction:

- event date and address;
- permits and insurance;
- menu, pricing, allergens, and refund terms;
- sponsor and fiscal-sponsor claims;
- tax-deductibility language;
- historical and Muhammad Ali-related claims;
- youth images, stories, and testimonials;
- payments and delivery-platform links;
- production domain changes.

## Durable lessons from prior nonprofit builds

- Do not let a website imply operational maturity that does not exist.
- Do not publish an event before date, venue, owner, registration, safety, and cancellation are confirmed.
- Separate parents, participants, donors, food customers, volunteers, sponsors, and partners into distinct journeys.
- Preserve exact approved client language where legal, privacy, or trust wording matters.
- Verify mobile, forms, domain, deployment, and database behavior independently.
- Never allow visual assets to substitute for program design.
